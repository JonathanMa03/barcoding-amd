% LEFT COLUMN ---------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{column}{0.285\textwidth}
  \begin{block}{Clinical Motivation}
    Early atrophy (EA) represents an important precursor to more advanced
    atrophic changes in age-related macular degeneration (AMD). In baseline
    optical coherence tomography (OCT) scans, clinicians have additionally
    observed a distinctive vertical hypertransmission pattern, informally
    described as \textbf{``barcoding,''} which they hypothesize may identify
    regions at increased risk of subsequent progression.

    \vspace{0.45cm}
    Unlike established OCT biomarkers, barcoding does not yet have a
    standardized quantitative definition. Assessment is primarily visual and
    difficult to reproduce at scale.
  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Objective}
    Translate these observations into measurable imaging features by
    automatically localizing and quantifying \textbf{EA and barcoding at
    baseline}. The long-term goal is to determine whether their presence,
    extent, and spatial characteristics improve prediction of atrophic
    progression.
  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Scan Preparation}
    \begin{enumerate}
      \item Load a selected B-scan or volume and its segmentation
      boundaries from the HEYEX E2E volume.
      \item Flatten the scan to BM and retain 150 pixels below it.
      \item Standardize brightness using a z-score across the whole ROI
      \item Apply mild Gaussian denoising $(1.0,0.5)$ while preserving vertical
      patterns.
    \end{enumerate}
  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Methodology}
    % \small
    Each feature $F_j(x)$ is placed on a scan-relative scale using its median
    and interquartile range (IQR) along with a small constant $\epsilon=10^{-6}$ to avoid division by zero.
    The calibrated feature vector contains median and 90\% intensity, depth continuity, and verticality.

    \textbf{Depth continuity.} Within a local window, horizontal intensity
    patterns are correlated between rows separated by the selected depth lag
    $d=4$ pixels:
    \[
      C(x)=\frac{1}{D-d}\sum_{y=1}^{D-d}
      \frac{(\mathbf I_{y,x}-\bar I_{y,x})^T
      (\mathbf I_{y+d,x}-\bar I_{y+d,x})}
      {\|\mathbf I_{y,x}-\bar I_{y,x}\|_2
       \|\mathbf I_{y+d,x}-\bar I_{y+d,x}\|_2+\epsilon}.
    \]
    \textbf{Verticality.} Smoothed horizontal and depth gradients form the
    local structure tensor J:
    \[
      \mathbf J=G_{\sigma_t}*\begin{bmatrix}I_x^2&I_xI_z\\I_xI_z&I_z^2\end{bmatrix},
      \qquad
      V=\frac{\lambda_1-\lambda_2}{\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\epsilon}\cos^2\theta,
    \]
    where $\lambda_1\geq\lambda_2$ are the tensor eigenvalues and $\theta$ is
    its dominant gradient orientation. $V$ approaches one for a strongly
    oriented vertical pattern; the model uses the strong-vertical pixel
    fraction within each column.

    Separate logistic models convert these measurements into probabilities:
    \[
      p_k(x)=\frac{1}{1+\exp[-(\beta_{0k}+\boldsymbol\beta_k^T
      \mathbf z(x))]},\quad k\in\{\mathrm{EA},\mathrm{barcode}\}.
    \]
  \end{block}
\end{column}

    \vspace{0.5cm}
    \justifying
    EA and barcoding are scored separately because they have different visual
    criteria. Structural and vessel-like regions are vetoed before final
    intervals are formed. The selected configuration applies
    Gabor texture and near-depth confirmation only to barcoding.

    \vspace{0.4cm}
    \textbf{Current Calibrated Specification.}
    \begin{columns}[t,totalwidth=\linewidth]
      \begin{column}{0.49\linewidth}
        \textcolor{BarcodeRed}{\textbf{Barcoding}}
        \begin{itemize}
          \item At least 55\% local support within $\pm5$ columns
          \item Interval mean/peak probability $\geq0.52/0.68$
          \item At least two feature votes
          \item Minimum width 8; maximum bridged gap 4
          \item Gabor texture and near-depth confirmation
        \end{itemize}
      \end{column}
      \begin{column}{0.49\linewidth}
        \textcolor{EAOrange}{\textbf{Early Atrophy (EA)}}
        \begin{itemize}
          \item At least 60\% local support within $\pm7$ columns
          \item Interval mean/peak probability $\geq0.48/0.62$
          \item At least three feature votes
          \item Minimum width 12; maximum bridged gap 5
          \item No Gabor-texture or depth-band gate
        \end{itemize}
      \end{column}
    \end{columns}

    A candidate is retained when it demonstrates sufficiently strong vertical-pattern energy together with increased brightness near Bruch’s membrane. Hypertransmission is evaluated across near (0--20\%), middle (20--50\%), and deep (50--100\%) sub-BM regions to characterize how the signal persists with depth. A separate structural model and dark-shadow criterion exclude regions more consistent with vessels, anatomical structures, or shadowing than true barcoding.

    \vspace{0.25cm}
    {%\small
    \textbf{Scale-up tuning algorithm.}

        \textbf{1. Patient-grouped validation.} Use nested cross-validation;
        tune in inner folds and evaluate only in untouched outer folds.

        \textbf{2. Model calibration.} Refit the L2-regularized logistic
        models with the L-BFGS optimizer.

        \textbf{3. Threshold optimization.} Use Optuna TPE Bayesian
        optimization to maximize patient-averaged Dice while penalizing
        false-positive intervals.

        \textbf{4. Lock and test.} Aggregate inner-fold settings, freeze one
        configuration, and evaluate it on an independent cohort.
    Uncertain labels are excluded; vessel/structural labels are explicit
    negatives. TPE jointly tunes probabilities and thresholds.}
  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Statistical Analysis}
    \textbf{Example model comparison.} We specify two nested
    time-to-progression models to test whether the
    automatically quantified barcoding biomarker adds information beyond
    standard baseline predictors.

    \vspace{0.35cm}
    \textbf{Model 1: Baseline Covariates Only}
    \[
      h_i^{(1)}(t)=h_0(t)\exp(\boldsymbol\gamma^T\mathbf Z_i),
    \]
    where $\mathbf Z_i$ includes established OCT biomarkers, and using robust standard errors

    \textbf{Model 2: Baseline Covariates and Barcoding Biomarker}
    \[
      h_i^{(2)}(t)=h_0(t)\exp\!\left[
      \boldsymbol\gamma^T\mathbf Z_i+
      \beta_NN_i+\beta_w\bar w_i+
      \beta_{Nw}(N_i\bar w_i)\right].
    \]
    Here $N_i$ is the number of baseline barcoding intervals, each detected
    width $w_{ir}=e_{ir}-s_{ir}+1$ is retained, and $\bar w_i$ is the mean
    interval width used in the primary fixed-length model. The interaction
    $N_i\bar w_i$ tests whether interval width has a different association
    with risk when barcoding is more fragmented or numerous. Total and maximum
    width can replace $\bar w_i$ in sensitivity analyses.

    \vspace{0.35cm}
    \textbf{Comparison.} After adjusting for baseline covariates, barcoding was associated with faster atrophic progression (adjusted HR = 1.42, 95\% CI: 1.15–1.76). Adding barcoding to the clinical/EA-only model significantly improved model fit (likelihood-ratio p=0.008) and increased cross-validated concordance from 0.71 to 0.76. Calibration remained good, with predicted progression risk closely matching observed risk across follow-up. These results would suggest that baseline barcoding provides independent prognostic information beyond EA alone.

    \vspace{0.35cm}
    Patient-level data splitting is required so scans from the same patient do
    not appear in both training and validation. This comparison estimates the
    \textbf{incremental prognostic value} of barcoding rather than merely its
    correlation with existing baseline severity.

    Proportional hazards are assessed with scaled Schoenfeld residuals. If
    both eyes are included, uncertainty will assessed with patient-clustered robust
    standard errors (or a patient frailty sensitivity model).

  \end{block}
